# OPC asset and IP preparation checklist

Load this reference for a complete pre-launch review. It contains issue-spotting prompts, not legal conclusions.

## Input evidence

### Product name and domain

- Exact spelling, language variants, logo, intended goods or services, and target users;
- name-search evidence from the appropriate official registry;
- domain registrant, registrar, administrative account, renewal contact, and recovery method;
- entity or founder currently controlling each asset.

### Code and content provenance

Classify each material source:

- founder or employee;
- contractor or partner;
- open-source dependency;
- purchased or licensed asset;
- commissioned work;
- AI-generated material;
- dataset, prompt, model output, or user submission.

Collect dated creation records, repository history, contracts, assignment or license terms, dependency licenses, invoices, consent, attribution, and account ownership where relevant.

### Partners and entity

- Identify every contributor and deliverable.
- Check written ownership, confidentiality, permitted use, payment, warranties, liability, and exit terms.
- Compare the intended company with the holder of domains, repositories, stores, payment accounts, filings, and contracts.
- Prepare a transfer or clarification question where the chain is incomplete; do not draft a definitive legal instrument without scope and professional review.

### Core algorithm or recipe

Describe the asset without unnecessarily disclosing the secret. Ask:

- Can a user or competitor reverse-engineer it from the product?
- Has it already been publicly disclosed?
- Can secrecy be maintained with real access controls and agreements?
- Is there a concrete technical solution rather than a business idea alone?
- What business advantage and lifespan justify the protection cost?
- Who created it and who owns it?

Use the answers to prepare a patent-versus-trade-secret question for a qualified professional; do not make the filing decision from this checklist alone.

## Timing tests

### Must act now

- a product name is about to be announced without clearance evidence;
- a contractor or partner ownership chain is unresolved;
- public disclosure may remove a protection option;
- critical domains or accounts are controlled by the wrong person or entity;
- required consent, license, official filing, or platform permission is missing for the planned launch;
- secrecy is claimed but no protective measures exist.

These are review triggers, not automatic legal conclusions. Verify the jurisdiction and facts.

### Can wait

Delay only when the option remains available, the asset is documented, the launch is not blocked, and a dated trigger is defined for revisiting the item.

### Professional review

Escalate jurisdiction-specific registrability, patentability, contract interpretation, privacy or consumer duties, employment or founder ownership, disputes, enforcement, and filing strategy. State what documents and facts the professional needs.
